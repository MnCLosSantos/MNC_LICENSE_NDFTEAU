## 12. CODE THEFT — WARNING & CONSEQUENCES

### 12.1 What Is Code Theft

For the purposes of this document, code theft refers to the
practice of taking code written by another developer — whether
copied verbatim, lightly modified, obfuscated, or restructured —
and presenting it as one's own original work, or distributing or
selling it in a way that removes or undermines the original
author's credit and rights.

Code theft is morally wrong regardless of its legal status. It
harms the developer who invested time and skill. It misleads
the community about who built what. It devalues the reputation
of everyone who publishes honest, credited work. It is also, in
virtually every case, copyright infringement.

### 12.2 Common Forms of Code Theft Observed in the Community

The FiveM development community has a documented problem with
code theft. Common patterns include:

- **Script resellers:** Taking free or leaked scripts and
  selling them on Tebex or Keymaster as their own work,
  sometimes with superficial changes and a new name.

- **Attribution stripping:** Downloading scripts, removing
  the author's name from comments and README files, and
  publishing to Discord or GitHub under a new identity.

- **"Edits" sold as originals:** Making minor modifications
  to existing scripts and presenting the result as an
  entirely new, original resource.

- **Incorporating code into larger paid scripts:** Using
  sections of free scripts as components in a paid product
  without credit or permission.

- **Leaking paid scripts:** Obtaining legitimately paid
  resources and distributing them freely or selling them
  independently.

All of these practices are prohibited under this License and
will be treated as infringement.

### 12.3 How Theft Is Detected

The Author is an active member of the FiveM development
community and monitors script releases, Discord servers,
Tebex storefronts, and other platforms for infringing content.
Additionally:

- The community frequently reports stolen scripts directly
  to original authors.
- Code comparison tools can identify structural similarity
  even in obfuscated or renamed scripts.
- Unique implementation choices, bugs, and comments function
  as fingerprints that survive superficial modification.
- The Author maintains relationships with other developers
  who share information about known bad actors.

If you have stolen code from this library, there is a
meaningful chance the Author will find out.

### 12.4 Consequences of Code Theft

In addition to the consequences listed in Section 11.6, code
theft may result in:

- Public identification of the stolen script and the
  individual or organisation responsible
- Notification to every platform where the individual
  sells or publishes resources
- Reports to FiveM/CFX resulting in forum bans, Keymaster
  account suspension, and loss of verified developer status
- Coordination with other developers whose work has been
  stolen by the same party to pursue coordinated action
- In cases involving sale for profit: formal legal proceedings
  for copyright infringement, unjust enrichment, and
  potentially fraud

### 12.5 A Direct Message to Those Considering Theft

If you are reading this and considering copying these scripts,
removing the credits, and publishing them as your own — whether
for clout, for money, or because you do not think anyone will
notice — consider the following:

These scripts took real time to write. Not hours. Days and
weeks spread across months of development, testing, debugging,
and refinement. The author behind them is a real person who
chose to share them with the community for free.

Taking them without credit is not a victimless act. It is
theft of time, effort, and reputation from a person who
chose generosity. It makes the community worse for everyone,
because developers who get burned by theft are less likely
to share their next project.

Do the right thing. If you use these scripts, say so. If you
want to share them, link to this repository. If you want to
modify them, go ahead — privately, on your own server,
with the credits intact. That is all that is being asked.
